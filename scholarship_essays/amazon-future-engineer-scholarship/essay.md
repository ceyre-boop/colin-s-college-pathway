# Amazon Future Engineer Scholarship

The software running on a radiation therapy treatment planning workstation was written by engineers I will never meet, and on the day I sat down at my uncle's clinic in North Dakota, I had nothing but a mouse cursor and a patient's scan between me and a finished treatment plan. I was 19. The tumor-segmentation workflow had not changed in the way everything else in medicine had changed, because the software problem is genuinely hard — not hard in the sense of "nobody's tried," but hard in the sense that if the model is wrong about where the tumor ends, the consequences are medical.

That is the software problem I want to work on.

I'm studying Cellular and Molecular Biology with optional CS / computational support courses at the University of Michigan-Flint — 3.92 GPA, Dean's List. Finishing prerequisites at Mott Community College (3.7) now, returning to UM-Flint Fall 2027. Nine AP exams, 32 transfer credits, AP Scholar with Distinction twice.

The CS work is already production-level. I built Alta Investments at 18 — a live quantitative trading system that runs daily on real capital with a Sharpe ratio of 1.08 and a risk model holding bust probability at zero. I'm the founding AI engineer at TABOOST, where the system I built processes 200-plus emails a day across 16 inboxes. Both systems required the same engineering discipline: design for the failure case first, instrument everything, and maintain the system under live conditions where the cost of being wrong is real.

That last point is the one I keep returning to in the context of medical AI. The engineering bar for software that participates in treatment decisions is categorically higher than the bar for software that loses a trade. Alta already runs at a bar where being wrong costs real money. The oncology work will require being even more careful about what the model knows versus what it doesn't — uncertainty quantification, explainable predictions, graceful degradation when a scan is ambiguous.

Anyway, the record of finishing is long: black belt at 11 after 6 years, Eagle Scout at 13 (top 4%), fourth at Georgia state wrestling on a 4.1. The pattern is not accidental. FAFSA SAI negative 1500, maximum Pell, and the funding gap is treated as an engineering constraint: Mott first, then UM-Flint where the labs are.

Amazon Future Engineer scholarship would go toward tuition and research access — the two things between me and the version of that workstation where the software proposes the boundary and the engineer gets to verify, instead of the other way around.
