# The NetApp Scholarship Program

An MRI scan is not one image. The brain scan I worked from at my uncle's radiation oncology clinic in North Dakota was 50-plus slices, each a separate grayscale file, loaded sequentially on a treatment planning workstation that took a few seconds per load. The total study was hundreds of megabytes. Across a busy clinic in a year, that is terabytes of imaging data that someone's treatment depends on being right, accessible, and consistent.

I am a Cellular and Molecular Biology major at the University of Michigan-Flint, computer science minor, 3.92 GPA with Dean's List standing. The long-term research goal is computational oncology — AI that reads the tumor boundary so the physician verifies rather than draws. Getting there requires understanding the data layer as well as the biological one: where medical imaging data lives, how it moves, what happens when storage fails or the transfer is corrupted, and how to build systems that produce reproducible results from a data pipeline that runs through multiple clinical handoffs.

The data infrastructure work I do now is production-grade. Alta Investments, a quantitative trading system I built solo starting at 18, runs a live data feed through a signal-processing and decision pipeline every trading day — Sharpe 1.08, bust probability 0%. At TABOOST I'm the founding AI engineer; the AI inbox system I built ingests, classifies, and routes 200-plus emails a day across 16 inboxes. Both systems fail publicly if the data handling fails. The standards they require are the same standards clinical data systems require.

Anyway, I've been finishing long things since before the code. Black belt at 11 after 6 years of training. Eagle Scout at 13, top 4% of all scouts. Fourth in Georgia state wrestling my junior year while carrying a 4.1. The pattern repeats: pick the hard thing, learn it deeply enough to be reliable at it, stay until the work is done.

The academic path reflects the same discipline. Mott Community College now for prerequisite credits (3.7), returning to UM-Flint Winter 2027. FAFSA SAI of negative 1500 — maximum Pell eligibility — and I treated the funding gap the same way I treat data pipeline errors: find the efficient path to correct output. Mott is the lower-cost route to the same transfer credits. The gap is just a variable.

The NetApp scholarship would buy back the hours I spend managing the cost structure and redirect them toward the coursework and research that connects the data science and the biology. What I'm building at the end is an imaging AI system — and that starts with understanding what medical imaging data is and how to handle it at scale.
