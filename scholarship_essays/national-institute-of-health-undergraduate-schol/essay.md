# NIH Undergraduate Scholarship Program

The scan was already on the screen when I sat down — a brain in grayscale, sliced thin. My job at my uncle's radiation oncology clinic was simple to describe and brutal to do: move the cursor around the tumor's edge, slice after slice, so the radiation plan would know where to aim. I was 19. By the third slice I had stopped thinking about the software and started thinking about the person, and I have not really stopped since.

That afternoon turned a vague interest in computation into a specific research goal: computational oncology. I'm pursuing it the way the problem deserves — from both sides. Cellular and Molecular Biology major with optional CS / computational support courses, currently finishing prerequisites at Mott Community College before returning to the University of Michigan-Flint in Fall 2027, where my GPA is 3.92 with Dean's List standing. The biology tells you what a tumor is. The computation tells you where it ends. Treatment lives on that boundary, and right now a human hand draws it.

I come from the exact financial situation this program exists for. My FAFSA SAI is negative 1500 — maximum Pell eligibility. I answered that constraint the way I answer most things: I treated the gap as a variable and engineered around it, which is what Mott is. Same credits, fraction of the cost, zero drift from the goal.

And I can already do the computational half at production grade. At TABOOST I'm the founding AI engineer; the system I built processes 200+ emails a day across 16 inboxes for real users. At 18 I founded Alta Investments, a quantitative trading system that runs live with a Sharpe ratio of 1.08 and a risk model holding bust probability at 0%. Markets and tumors are different domains with the same skeleton: noisy data, high stakes, and a decision that has to be defensible. I've built the pipeline-to-decision loop once. I want to build it where the output is a treatment plan instead of a trade.

Anyway, research careers are long, and I have a record of staying. Six years of martial arts for a black belt at 11. Eagle Scout at 13, top 4% of scouts. Fourth place at Georgia state wrestling while carrying a 4.1. Nine AP exams. The same persistence is now pointed at one problem.

What I want from the next decade is concrete: a lab that works on medical imaging, a PhD-track foundation in biomedical research, and models that read scans the way I had to — except faster, more consistently, and for every patient instead of the ones whose clinic has spare hands. The NIH scholarship would let me spend my remaining undergraduate years inside that work rather than adjacent to it.

I traced tumors with a mouse cursor once. I intend to spend my career replacing it.
