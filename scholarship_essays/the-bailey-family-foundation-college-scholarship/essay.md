# The Bailey Family Foundation College Scholarship

My FAFSA SAI is negative fifteen hundred. That's the whole financial section of this essay, and I'd rather spend the rest of it on something more interesting.

So here's the thing I've been turning over. Almost every program built for isolated kids works by moving them. You find the student eating alone and you walk him over to the big table, and everyone calls that inclusion. Lunch Bunch goes the other way. The table doesn't move. The general students come to it.

I run that program, which in practice means making sure the table exists on a Tuesday and that somebody is already sitting there before the first person shows up — because nobody wants to be the one who starts a table. That's most of the job. Arrive early, sit down, stay put.

It took me a while to work out why the reversal matters as much as it does, and I'm still not sure I have all of it. The best I can say is this: it changes who has to cross the room. When you walk the isolated kid over, the arrangement is a favor and everybody in it knows so. When you make people come to him, he's the host. Nobody announces the difference. It just quietly stops being charity.

I keep finding the same shape in things I build. At TABOOST I wrote the system that reads and routes two hundred emails a day across sixteen inboxes for creators who needed that infrastructure and had no way to build it. It works when nobody notices it — that's the specification, not a side effect. Good infrastructure is invisible and so is a table that was already there.

Community college was the same kind of unglamorous arithmetic. Same prerequisites, a fraction of the cost, and I transfer back to UM-Flint in Fall 2027 to finish the biology degree. The gap between what I have and what this costs is just a variable, and variables get solved.

The last thing I'll say is about a room in North Dakota. I was sixteen, at my uncle's radiation oncology clinic, and he let me outline a tumor on an MRI — drag the cursor around a gray smudge, one slice, then the next, forty more waiting. Somewhere on the other side of that smudge was a person with an appointment. It took eleven minutes to do one slice by hand.

I want to build the thing that draws that line, so the doctor checks it instead of tracing it. It'll take years. I'm going to be early and I'm going to stay put.
