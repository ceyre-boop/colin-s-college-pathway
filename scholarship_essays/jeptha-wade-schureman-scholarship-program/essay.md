# Jeptha Wade Schureman Scholarship Program

The University of Michigan-Flint sits in a city that has spent the last decade demonstrating what happens when infrastructure fails the people who depend on it. I know that story from the inside — not from the water crisis directly, but from living and studying in a region where the gap between what institutions promise and what they deliver is not abstract.

That context is part of why I think about the gap between current medical technology and what it could be as a local problem, not just a research problem.

I'm at Mott Community College right now, carrying a 3.7 on prerequisite credits, returning to UM-Flint in Winter 2027 for a BS in Cellular and Molecular Biology with a computer science minor. My cumulative GPA at UM-Flint is 3.92, Dean's List. The plan runs through Southeast Michigan by design: Mott is cost-efficient for the prerequisites, and UM-Flint's biology program has the coursework I need without the cost that relocating to a research university would carry. FAFSA SAI of negative 1500 — maximum Pell — means the path optimization matters. Southeast Michigan is where the path is.

The computational work I do alongside the coursework: Alta Investments is a live quantitative trading system I founded at 18, running a Sharpe ratio of 1.08 with bust probability at zero. At TABOOST, I'm the founding AI engineer; the routing system I built handles 200-plus emails a day across 16 inboxes for real users. These are not hypothetical — they run every day.

What they're building toward started at 19, at my uncle's radiation oncology clinic in North Dakota. I spent part of an afternoon outlining tumors on MRI scans — moving a cursor around the tumor boundary, slice by slice, knowing the line I drew was part of someone's treatment plan. The technology that automates that process — computational oncology, AI-proposed boundaries, physician-verified — does not adequately exist yet, and I have been building toward it since.

Southeast Michigan needs the kind of technical research investment that starts with people who come from here and have a reason to stay. I'm not going to Flint because it was the practical option; I'm going because UM-Flint is a solid university in a community that has concrete, unsolved problems, and I'd rather solve problems in a place I know than study them from a distance.

The Schureman scholarship, given to Southeast Michigan students who are building something serious, would reduce the financial distance between the current path and the research lab where the work I've described actually begins.
