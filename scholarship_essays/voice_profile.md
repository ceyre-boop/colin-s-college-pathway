# Colin's Voice Profile — Scholarship Essays

How to write (and judge) an essay so it sounds like Colin and not like a press release.
Every essay in this directory was drafted against this profile. When you edit one, keep it inside these lines.

---

## The voice, from actual writing samples

- Writes in **lowercase**, rarely capitalizes unless emphasizing. *(Submission essays use standard capitalization — see "Capitalization decision" below — but the rhythm underneath stays his.)*
- **Outcome-first** — leads with the result, explains after. The number lands in sentence one, the story explains it in sentence three.
- **Short punchy sentences mixed with longer run-ons** connected by "and" and "so". The variation is the style; don't smooth it out.
- Uses **"anyway"** to transition between sections or to pull out of a tangent.
- **Self-aware without being self-deprecating in a weak way.** He'll admit what he didn't know, but the sentence ends with what he did about it.
- **References his own work as proof, not as bragging.** The project is evidence for a claim, never the trophy.
- **Never uses corporate phrases**: "passionate about", "making a difference", "excited to contribute", "honored to", "in today's world". If a sentence could open a LinkedIn post, cut it.
- **Specific. Names exact numbers**: Sharpe 1.08, 200+ emails a day, 16 inboxes, 0% bust probability, 6 years of martial arts, 3.92 GPA, SAI −1500.
- **The MRI moment is his north star** — outlining tumors on his uncle's scans at a radiation oncology clinic. Essays return to it; it's the why behind everything.
- **Connects everything back to one concrete future**: building the AI that replaces the mouse cursor he used at 19 to outline tumors on his uncle's scans.

## Voice rules for essays

1. **Open with a specific sensory moment — not a thesis.**
2. **Numbers over adjectives every time.**
3. The word "I" can start a sentence — but never **"I am passionate"**.
4. **End on what he's building, not what he hopes to become.**
5. **Maximum one metaphor per essay**, and make it count.
6. **Read-it-aloud test**: if it sounds like a press release, rewrite it.

## Capitalization decision

Colin's raw style is lowercase. These essays go to scholarship committees, so they use **standard sentence capitalization** (confirmed 2026-06-11). Everything else — rhythm, run-ons, "anyway", outcome-first ordering — is unchanged. If a committee or platform is informal (Bold.org-style), lowercasing an essay back is a one-pass edit; recapitalizing isn't.

## Smell test (run before saving any essay)

- Does sentence one contain something you can see, hear, or touch?
- Could any sentence survive on a corporate careers page? Rewrite it.
- Count the metaphors. More than one? Cut the weaker one.
- Does it end on a thing he's building?
- Read it aloud. Press release? Start over.
- Word count 400–500.
