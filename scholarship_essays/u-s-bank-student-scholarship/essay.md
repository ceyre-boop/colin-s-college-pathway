# U.S. Bank Student Scholarship

My uncle did not usually let people observe treatment planning. He made an exception — I had been asking for years — and what I found when I sat down was a problem I had not expected: the machine was waiting for a human hand to draw the boundary.

The scan was on the screen. Brain, cross-sectioned in grayscale, forty-plus slices. My job was to move the cursor around the tumor's edge on each slice, marking where the tissue changed. I was 19. It took eleven minutes to outline one slice. There were forty more.

That afternoon did more to clarify my future than anything in four years of AP coursework, because the problem was obvious and the solution was not: somewhere between the radiologist who does not have time to trace every boundary and the patient who needs accurate targeting, there is a software problem that has not been properly solved. Computational oncology is what I have been building toward since.

The academic path runs through Cellular and Molecular Biology with optional CS / computational support courses at the University of Michigan-Flint — 3.92 GPA, Dean's List. I'm finishing prerequisites at Mott Community College (3.7) now, returning to UM-Flint Fall 2027. Nine AP exams and AP Scholar with Distinction twice gave me 32 transfer credits — a head start I used to reach upper-division coursework faster than a standard four-year path.

The computational side is already production-grade. Alta Investments, which I built solo starting at 18, is a live quantitative trading system running a Sharpe ratio of 1.08 with bust probability modeled at zero. As founding AI engineer at TABOOST, my system reads and routes 200-plus emails a day across 16 inboxes for real users. These are not class projects — they run every day and fail in public if I let them slip. The discipline they require is the same discipline the oncology software will need: correct output, every time, on data you cannot afford to misread.

Anyway, the funding situation is real. My FAFSA SAI is negative 1500 — maximum Pell eligibility. I've treated that constraint the way I treat every constraint: Mott is the efficient path to the same credits at a fraction of the cost, and the gap is just a variable, not the story. The story is where this ends.

The U.S. Bank scholarship would accelerate a path that is already moving — tuition and fees redirected away from the funding gap and toward the coursework and lab access that get me from prerequisites to the research where the boundary problem actually lives. The goal is fixed: software that reads a tumor margin and lets a physician verify instead of draw. I'm going to build it either way. This would help me build it faster.
