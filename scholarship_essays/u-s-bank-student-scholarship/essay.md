# U.S. Bank Student Scholarship

My FAFSA SAI is negative fifteen hundred. That's the number, and I'd rather use the rest of this on what I did with it than on how it feels.

What I did was treat it like a constraint instead of a verdict, which sounds like a slogan until you see what it actually costs you. It meant sitting down and separating the parts of a degree that are load-bearing from the parts that are just where people usually go. Organic chemistry is load-bearing. The letterhead on the building isn't. So I'm at Mott Community College finishing prerequisites for a fraction of the price and transferring back to UM-Flint in Fall 2027 for the biology degree, which is where the labs I actually need are.

Two years of the same credits, at maybe a fifth of the cost, at the price of explaining myself in essays like this one. I'd make that trade every time.

The reason I think in constraints at all is that I built a business inside one. At eighteen I wrote Alta Investments, a quantitative trading system that runs on live capital by itself every trading day. The first component I built wasn't the strategy — it was the risk model, the thing that decides when the system is not allowed to act, which I ground down to zero probability of ruin before I let it touch a dollar. When you have a small amount of money and no one to bail you out, survival isn't a feature you add later. It's the design.

That's the same math as my financial aid situation, honestly. Protect the downside, spend only where it compounds, and don't confuse looking successful with being solvent.

None of it is the point, though. The point is a room in North Dakota.

The scan was on the screen when I sat down — a brain in grayscale, forty-plus slices. I was sixteen, at my uncle's radiation oncology clinic, and my job was to move the cursor around the tumor's edge on each slice so the radiation plan would know where to aim. Eleven minutes for one. Forty more waiting. Behind the smudge was a person with a Thursday appointment.

The biology degree and the code are both pointed at that: software that proposes the boundary so the oncologist verifies instead of traces.

The gap between what this costs and what I have is just a variable. I've solved harder ones with less.

I'd just rather spend the next four years solving that room.
