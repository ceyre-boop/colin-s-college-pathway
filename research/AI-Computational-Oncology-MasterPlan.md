## WHAT THE FIELD ACTUALLY IS

AI Computational Oncology sits at the intersection of three things you already do: machine learning/AI (TABOOST, quant systems), rigorous hypothesis-driven analysis (Alta Investments methodology), and biology (CMB degree, uncle's radiation oncology clinic). It is not one job — it's a cluster of specializations, and where you land inside it changes the required training and the daily work.

### The Five Specializations, Ranked by Fit for You

**1. AI & Imaging Analytics — Your strongest natural fit**
Deep learning applied to radiology scans, histopathology slides, and multi-modal clinical data for diagnosis and classification. This is pattern recognition at scale — structurally identical to what you already do reading chart patterns and market microstructure. The uncle's-clinic MRI moment (outlining tumor boundaries by hand) is literally the problem this field automates.

**2. Computational Genomics & Bioinformatics**
Analyzing DNA/RNA sequencing data — tumor mutations, gene expression, single-cell sequencing — to track cancer evolution and find drug targets. This is the "wet lab meets data science" track. Most direct overlap with your CMB coursework (genetics, cell biology).

**3. Computational Immuno-Oncology**
Focuses on cancer-immune system interface — predicting immunotherapy response, analyzing immune cell infiltration. Hot field right now (checkpoint inhibitors, CAR-T, cancer vaccines all live here).

**4. Mechanistic & Mathematical Modeling**
Physics/biology-based equations simulating tumor growth, angiogenesis, metastasis. Heaviest math requirement — differential equations, PDEs. Closest to a physics degree with a biology application.

**5. Agent-Based Modeling & Digital Twins**
Simulating individual cells as independent agents; building personalized virtual tumor models to test treatments before applying them. Newest, most speculative, smallest current job market — but potentially the biggest long-term payoff as compute gets cheaper.

**Your path likely blends #1 and #2** — imaging/AI as the technical core, genomics as the biological substrate. That combination is exactly what Tempus AI, PathAI, and the academic labs doing this work are hiring for.

## THE EXACT PROGRAM — VERIFIED CURRENT DATA

### University of Michigan PIBS (Program in Biomedical Sciences)

This is your target. It's the umbrella application that gates entry into 14 PhD programs, including the two most relevant to you:

**Track A: Bioinformatics PhD** — housed in the Department of Computational Medicine & Bioinformatics (DCMB). 87 PhD students, 120+ Master's students, 130 affiliated faculty across Medical School, Engineering, LSA, Public Health, School of Information. Direct coursework in machine learning, genomics, proteomics, clinical informatics.

**Track B: Cancer Biology PhD** — required core courses in Cancer Biology AND Bioinformatics. Two-checkpoint preliminary exam. This track exists specifically for people going after the oncology angle you care about — you could potentially straddle both tracks or pick whichever lab fits once you're in.

**Key facts, verified current:**
- **No GRE required**
- Application opens **late August**, deadline **December 1** for the following fall
- **Zero undergraduate biology requirement is NOT a barrier** — quantitatively trained students with no college biology attend a 2-week "Biology Boot Camp" before term starts. But you'll walk in with a full CMB degree, so this doesn't apply to you — you'll be ahead of most incoming cohorts.
- Students rotate through labs in year one before picking a permanent mentor — this means you don't have to know your exact sub-specialization before you apply, just the general direction (cancer + computation).
- **Funding: $43,788/year stipend + full tuition + health/dental insurance**, guaranteed for the duration of the PhD as long as you're in good academic standing. Compare this to the $71,759–$94,139 cost of attendance a self-funded student would pay — you pay **zero** and get paid instead.
- PIBS Preview program: an on-campus recruiting weekend for students planning to start Fall 2027 or Fall 2028 — **UM pays for your travel, lodging, and food to attend.** This is worth applying to in your junior year (2028-29) as a low-risk way to visit campus and meet faculty before you formally apply.

**MS-holders can waive up to 6 credit hours** if coursework overlaps — not directly relevant to you since you're coming straight from a BS, but worth knowing the CS minor coursework may reduce required credits once you're in.

## THE RISK-ADJUSTED CASE — WHY THIS BEATS DERMATOLOGY FOR YOU SPECIFICALLY

You already articulated the core fear correctly: randomness derailing a long plan. Here's the structural comparison:

**Hard gates that can end the dermatology path:** MCAT score, medical school admission, Step 1 score, Step 2 score, the dermatology match itself (one of the most competitive matches in all of medicine — most applicants who don't match are extremely qualified and still don't get in). Any one of these failing sends you down a completely different, less lucrative specialty.

**Hard gates that can end the computational oncology path:** none that are structurally terminal. If a specific PhD program doesn't work out, you leave with a Master's and go to industry. If a specific lab rotation doesn't fit, you rotate to another. If life interrupts — family emergency, health issue, financial pressure — the work is portable, often remote-capable, and doesn't require maintaining a medical license or completing a fixed-length residency clock.

**The evidence you already have about yourself:** you built the Alta Investments quant system — hypothesis ledgers, out-of-sample testing, systematic killing of your own bad ideas — voluntarily, for years, because you find it genuinely engaging. That is not speculation about future job satisfaction. That's four years of revealed preference. Nobody has ever had to make you do that work.
