## Three corrections to the starting assumptions

**1. Your grades are not what's holding you back.** You have 15 credits at 3.92 (UM-Flint, Dean's List) and 15 credits at 3.70 (Mott). That's about a **3.81 cumulative**, which is in range for highly selective transfer admission. What's weaker is recent rigor. Fall 2026 has one STEM course (Calc I). The current Winter 2027 plan is public speaking, guitar and an open elective. Transfer committees read your most recent semesters hardest.

**2. The most selective schools are usually the cheapest for you.** An SAI of -1500 puts you in the lowest bracket every elite private school uses. Harvard, MIT, Penn, Princeton and Stanford all advertise free tuition well above your likely family income, and most of them cover housing and food too for families under about $75k–$150k. These policies don't depend on Michigan residency. The money problem is mostly solved by getting in. Athletic money isn't needed, and the Ivy League doesn't award it anyway.

**3. Residency only matters for the University of Michigan campuses.** If you get in-state status, UM Ann Arbor plus the Go Blue Guarantee means free tuition (families up to $125k income and $125k assets, all three campuses). For computational oncology specifically, Ann Arbor is as good as most Ivies: Rogel Cancer Center, DCMB, and PIBS, the PhD program you already plan to apply to. Ann Arbor isn't "settling".

## Decide residency first. It changes the plan.

Facts from UM's own guidelines (UM sets its own residency rules, separate from the state's):

- You need **clear and convincing evidence** that Michigan is your permanent home. A 12-month stay doesn't count by itself.
- **If your parents permanently live in Michigan, you're eligible immediately.** That's the easiest path.
- Otherwise, the main path is a **full-time, permanent job in Michigan that is the main reason you're here**, plus cutting the ties that point to another state.
- **Temporary jobs, and jobs students normally hold, don't count.**

**Verdict on the gap semester:** your instinct points the right way, but the form is wrong. A full gap semester helps the residency case and hurts the elite applications. They see no Winter 2027 grades, and that semester's midterm report is part of the file.

**Better: work full-time and take 1–2 hard classes part-time at Mott** (Calc II, General Chemistry II, or an intro programming course). That reads as "employed Michigander who also takes classes", and it gives admissions recent STEM rigor. Before you register, call the UM Residency Classification Office (734-764-1400) and describe this exact setup.

**Paper trail, starting now:** Michigan driver's license, voter registration and vehicle registration. A lease in your own name. File 2026 taxes as a Michigan resident. A Michigan bank account. A letter from your employer saying the job is full-time and permanent. Drop the Georgia equivalents.

If UM residency fails, the private schools don't care, so the portfolio still works.

## The essay

The transfer essay answers one question: **why leave, and why us.** Your true story already answers it:

> Georgia → Michigan. Dean's List at UM-Flint. Left for Mott on purpose because it was free. Building production AI at a real company while doing it. Now needs a research university to turn the MRI-contouring moment into a career.

Rules:
- **Never write "I don't like Mott."** Mott was a deliberate financial decision. Your own timeline says so ("Mott pays me").
- **Your "I only perform as well as the competition" trait is the essay.** Said plainly: *I rise to the room I'm in, and I want a harder room.* That's a reason to transfer, not a weakness.
- **You have to write it yourself.** Every essay in `scholarship_essays/` is an AI draft you've never revised (see `Plans/colin-ai-personal-voice-model-*.md`). The Common App treats submitting someone else's writing as your own as fraud. Use the AI drafts and the voice tools for structure and feedback only, not as the submission.

## Athletics: honest take

- **Ivy League schools give zero athletic scholarships.** A coach can support an admission, but for a transfer applicant two years past his last match, the odds are low.
- Partial athletic scholarships at small schools are worth less than the need-based aid you'd get at the schools above.
- **The one cheap move:** a 20-minute email with your results to the wrestling coaches at Cornell, Penn and Princeton. It's a free lottery ticket. Don't spend more on it than that.

## Scholarship pipeline: what changes

- **Jack Kent Cooke Transfer ($55k) — you're ineligible.** JKCF excludes anyone who has attended a four-year school, unless they did less than one full-time semester there. Fall 2025 at UM-Flint rules you out. (The cycle also closes Dec 9, 2026.) It was #1 in the June queue. `scout/eligibility.ts` now has a `prior-four-year` rule to screen out awards like it.
- **Through March, transfer applications beat $1k–$25k national scholarships on expected value.** One admission to a full-need school is worth more than the entire scout queue. Keep the scout running, but spend your hours on the applications.
- **Worth doing anyway:** PTK-linked transfer scholarships, UM internal awards, and Genesee County / Community Foundation of Greater Flint awards (small applicant pools).
